System Acquisition and Development

Transforming Business Operations: Strategic Choices in Information Systems

Acquiring or engineering an information system is never merely a technical purchase—it is a deliberate form of planned organizational change driven by Information Technology. Organizations must carefully balance strategic fit, financial risk, operational disruption, and long-term agility. Before committing to acquisition or development, organizations apply Business Process Management (BPM) to optimize foundational workflows.

BUSINESS PROCESS MANAGEMENT (BPM)

Methodologies used by firms to analyze, design, and optimize business processes.

Automation

Replaces manual tasks with automated workflows to increase operational efficiency.

The most common form of IT change. Calculates data faster, eliminates human calculation errors, and processes high-volume transactions instantly.

Real-World Case: Automated invoice matching.
Risk Profile: Lowest organizational risk.
⚙️

Rationalization

Level 2 · Standard Procedures
Tap to flip

Rationalization

Streamlines standard operating procedures to eliminate bottlenecks.

Often executed via TQM or Six Sigma. Standardizes procedures across business units to minimize unnecessary bureaucratic hops.

Real-World Case: Unified multi-department purchase approvals.
Risk Profile: Moderate risk.
🔄

Process Redesign

Level 3 · Workflow Transformation
Tap to flip

Process Redesign

Analyzes, simplifies, and reorganizes workflows to eliminate repetition.

Business Process Reengineering (BPR) cuts across silos, collapsing multi-person handoffs into single, empowered roles.

Real-World Case: Ford’s invoiceless processing.
Risk Profile: High risk; cultural resistance.
🚀

Paradigm Shift

Level 4 · Business Model Transform
Tap to flip

Paradigm Shift

Rethinks the fundamental nature of the business and redefines the model.

The most profound level of change. Transforms the entire value proposition and creates entirely new digital industry categories.

Real-World Case: Amazon bookstore to AWS.
Risk Profile: Highest risk; highest payoff.

Takeaway

System Acquisition & Development Synthesis

01. Planned Change Technology is a Business Catalyst

Every system acquisition or build is an organizational transformation. Align the level of change (Automation vs. Paradigm Shift) with leadership risk appetite.

02. Buy vs. Build Competitive Advantage Rule

Buy COTS software for standard non-differentiating processes (payroll, accounting); Build custom proprietary code only when it yields a defensible market advantage.

03. SDLC Choice Waterfall vs. Agile Fit

Deploy Waterfall for fixed-contract, high-compliance systems where requirements are unbending; deploy Agile when rapid customer learning and speed-to-market dominate.

04. COTS Governance Avoid the Customization Trap

If COTS meets 80% of needs, adapt company procedures. Modifying source code voids vendor warranties and creates insurmountable future technical debt.
